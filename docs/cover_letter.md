Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "Chronotype in the Neural and Behavioural Evaluation of Decision Feedback: Converging ERP, Computational, and Fusion Evidence," for consideration in *Psychophysiology*.

This manuscript tests whether morningness-eveningness is expressed in feedback processing during risky decision-making. The work is well matched to *Psychophysiology* because it centers on feedback-locked ERP evidence, especially the posterior P300, while integrating behavioural dynamics, reinforcement-learning analyses, and leakage-safe predictive modelling to evaluate convergent evidence rather than relying on a single analysis stream.

The main contribution is a converging, internally validated account of chronotype-related feedback evaluation in a modest single EEG cohort. Morning and evening chronotypes differed robustly in the posterior P300 loss-minus-gain contrast; this effect was stable across participant-exclusion robustness analyses and a specification-curve analysis. Behavioural choice dynamics independently decoded chronotype, and fusing behavioural dynamics with validated ERP contrasts improved prediction beyond either modality alone, suggesting partly independent behavioural and neural information.

We have taken particular care to avoid overclaiming. Predictive analyses use participant-generalizing evaluation, fold-contained preprocessing, nested model selection where applicable, and permutation tests that rerun the full evaluation pipeline. The manuscript also reports honest negative and boundary results: FRN effects were not established, equivalence tests were underpowered to prove equivalence, end-to-end EEGNet did not decode chronotype from learned single-trial EEG embeddings, and single-trial P300-to-next-choice coupling did not differ by chronotype. These null and robustness results are included to define the scope of the claim transparently.

We believe the manuscript will interest *Psychophysiology* readers because it combines established ERP methodology with modern reproducibility safeguards for individual-differences EEG: leakage-safe modelling, permutation-clean inference, specification-curve robustness, Bayesian/equivalence checks for null claims, and a clear distinction between confirmatory neural evidence and exploratory computational mechanisms.
